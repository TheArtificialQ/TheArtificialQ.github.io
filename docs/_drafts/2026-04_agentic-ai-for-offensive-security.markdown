
I just found an article [RiskInsight | Agentic AI for Offensive Security](https://www.riskinsight-wavestone.com/en/2026/04/agentic-ai-for-offensive-security/) containing some practical case studies on autonomous AI pentesting with Strix framework. I'm playing with Strix a lot lately (I ran close to hundred tests with different LLM models against different targets) and I found this articel excelent. If you are interested in autonomous AI pentesting, then please read it. If it's too long for you, read at least the last section called "Where the architecture can improve" as it contains an excellent overview of the current state of these systems and I agreed with every word there. I also decided to write this post with few quotes from that article and my comments on them.

_"The results become genuinely impressive only when the system is paired with a state-of-the-art model."_
That's exactly my experience. I wrote "go big or go home" after my first few tests with Strix and I'm more and more convinced about this. During next week I'll publish a post with summaries of my testing and the message is crystal clear - using smaller models with these autonomous systems is just waste of time and money. The authors also mentioned that _"Smaller models occasionally show flashes of insight and solve challenges that stronger models miss."_, but I don't have this experience at all. I saw smaller models get lucky from time to time, but it was not systematic and I don't think it meant that they are better suited to some types of vulnerabilities.

The article contains comparision of results between different LLM models - it's not the core message of the article, but still
_"These results should be interpreted cautiously. For each model and each challenge, the benchmark was limited to at most two runs."_
From my experience, two runs give you really rough idea about model performance. The test plan which each model executes significantly differs between runs and the post actually mentions it later: _"The non-deterministic nature of generative models remains visible: two runs can produce substantially different findings and reports against the same target."_ I ran my tests three times for each model, and I'm not sure if even that was enough. I would be also interested, if the authors sent some special instructions to Strix and if so, if they tried to tailor the instructions for each model.

_"Surprisingly, despite Sonnet’s higher per-token price, overall session costs were comparable to GPT-5, Anthropic’s model compensated through greater token efficiency."_
I must admit, that I wasn't able to make the Sonnet working with Strix in any meaningful way. For example, one of my test with Sonnet ran for more than two hours, during which I had to 105 times (!!!) manually resume the test after Sonnet reported that "This request would exceed your organization's rate limit of 30,000 input tokens per minute". During this time it found 1 vulnerability of of 14. I stopped it when it consumed $20. The other tests with Sonnet were very similar.

_"High verbosity and limited stealth, which makes these systems poorly suited in their default form for red-team style end-to-end scenarios that require discretion and tradecraft. This can be improved through dedicated configuration, without however equaling human capabilities."_
Frankly, I cannot imagine running any autonomous (i.e. without human in the loop) AI agentic system against a production server, so usage for a Red Team is out of the question anyway. And just adding human-in-the-loop is not enough to mitigate all risks. First the authores has a good point when saying _"...pausing the tests and requesting a human approval may lead to a similar situation with coding agents, with “developer fatigue”, where users become too trusting and stop critically challenging the agent’s conclusions."_ On top of that _"And of course, any vulnerability at the LLM level, such as susceptibility to prompt injection or poisonning, could be leveraged to hijack the automated pentest workflow. Essentially, those autonomous tools, if deployed internally, should be regarded as critical assets, with high value for attackers."_ Because of all of this, I believe these tools are perfect fit for some automation tests executed during continuous integration process or for pentesting against test instances, but I would not dare to run them during Red Team engagement.

_"Browser-driven interactions have progressed, yet some application types remain difficult to assess autonomously, especially multi-window or thick-client environments where headless browser interaction may not be enough."_
_"These systems rarely build a deep understanding of business logic. Their outputs remain strongly aligned with generic OWASP-style patterns and may not challenge the real business risk or abuse scenarios in a sufficiently contextual way."_
